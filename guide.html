INTRODUCTION

Comprehensive & Accessible

Across Texas, K–12 teachers face severe instructional pacing fatigue managing over 14,000 discrete TEKS standards. SombraTEKS transforms the schoolyard tree canopy into an interdisciplinary outdoor laboratory—enabling teachers to fulfill 7 to 10 standards across science, math, history, and health in a single 45-minute lesson.

WE STRIVE · WE BELIEVE
IN A RESILIENT TOMORROW

Tailored to the Lower Rio Grande Valley

The Lower Rio Grande Valley—spanning Starr, Hidalgo, Willacy, and Cameron counties—faces extreme urban surface heat (120°F+ asphalt playgrounds) coupled with extraordinary native ecological richness. SombraTEKS harnesses native keystone species like the Honey Mesquite (Prosopis glandulosa), Texas Ebony (Ebenopsis ebano), and Anacahuita (Cordia boissieri) as living apparatuses for direct student measurement.

🌳 Sombra Sana: The Pediatric Heat Shield
Direct thermal imaging demonstrates a 25°F to 35°F reduction in ambient ground temperature beneath mature South Texas canopies, safeguarding students while unlocking empirical thermodynamic science.

TREE IDENTIFIER

RGV Native Tree Key by Feel & Leaf

Students identify keystone Tamaulipan Thornscrub canopy trees using sensory field indicators without electronic field guides:

Field Decision Flowchart
1. Compound Feather Leaves?
Tiny bipinnate leaflets adapted to minimize midday moisture transpiration.
YES ➔ Honey Mesquite or Texas Ebony
2. Rough Sandpaper Leaves?
Oval, coarse leaves with white trumpet-shaped flowers and sweet olive fruit.
YES ➔ Anacahuita (Wild Olive)
3. Woody Dark Seed Pods?
Thick, dark-brown segmented seed pods containing hard seeds used for jewelry.
Texas Ebony (Ébano)
4. Golden Pechita Pods?
Long sweet yellowish pods rich in protein and milled into traditional flour.
Honey Mesquite (Mezquite)
🌱 Pedagogical Principle:
Observational taxonomy builds empirical scientific classification (TEKS Sci 1.12A, 2.12B, 5.12B) while fostering cultural grounding in regional flora.
