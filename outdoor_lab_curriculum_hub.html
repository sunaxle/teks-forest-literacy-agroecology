📘 Teacher Instructional Guide: The Urban Microclimate & Transpiration Audit Grades 3–5
Standard Type Alignment Details
Science 5.12B Core 3D TEKS Model how matter cycles and energy flows in ecosystems; demonstrate how tree leaves use sunlight and water to transpire vapor and cool surrounding environments.
Math 5.4H Cross-Curricular Represent and solve perimeter and area problems ($A \approx 3.14 \times r^2$, pacing calibration factor).
Health 5.3A Health & PE Analyze environmental heat factors impacting personal health; calculate safe recess shade capacity.

5E Instructional Learning Sequence

🔴 1. ENGAGE (15 min) — The Asphalt Frying Pan Phenomenon

Gather students at the edge of the asphalt basketball court. Ask: "Why does asphalt feel like a frying pan (over 135°F), but sitting under an oak or mesquite feels like an air conditioner?" Introduce stomata and transpiration as a living water-pump.

🔵 2. EXPLORE (35 min) — The 6-Zone Infrared Audit & Pacing Calibration

Students calibrate their step pace (10-meter baseline), pace out 4 canopy shadow radii ($r_N, r_S, r_E, r_W$), and record 3 repeated surface temperature readings across asphalt, turf, sunlit grass, shade, bark, and leaves.

🟣 3. EXPLAIN (20 min) — Transpiration Harvest & Phase-Change Cooling

Harvest the sealed 1-gallon branch bags after 60 min. Measure accumulated liquid water in graduated cylinders (mL). Explain that evaporating water absorbs thermal energy, chilling surrounding air like human sweat.

🟠 4. ELABORATE (15 min) — Canopy Shade Area & Recess Heat Refuge

Calculate shaded cooling footprint: $\text{Area} = 3.14 \times (r_{\text{avg}})^2$. Determine how many students can safely seek thermal shelter (1.5 m² per student) away from dangerously hot asphalt (>104°F).

🟢 5. EVALUATE (15 min) — Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER) Report

Students finalize their printable field worksheets, graphing surface temperatures and synthesizing energy flow and human health protections in an evidence-based CER paragraph.

📘 Teacher Instructional Guide: Albedo, Shadow Geometry & Canopy Energy Flux Grades 6–8
Standard Type Alignment Details
Science 8.10B Core 3D TEKS Describe how natural events and human activities influence climate systems and atmospheric energy transfer, including surface albedo ($\alpha$) and radiation balance ($R_n$).
Math 8.7B Cross-Curricular Use right-triangle trigonometry proportions ($\tan\theta$) and clinometers to solve for tree height: $H = D \cdot \tan\theta + h_{\text{eye}}$.
Social Studies 8.11A History & Geography Analyze how human modifications of the physical environment (urbanization, impervious surfaces) alter settlement microclimates and quality of life.

5E Instructional Learning Sequence

🔴 1. ENGAGE (15 min) — The Albedo & Radiation Balance Hook

Compare black vs white metal cans in sunlight. Introduce Albedo ($\alpha = S_{\text{out}} / S_{\text{in}}$) and explain why dark asphalt converts 90% of solar radiation into scorching sensible heat ($H$).

🔵 2. EXPLORE (35 min) — Protractor Clinometer Forestry & Albedo Audit

Construct DIY protractor clinometers. Measure a 15-meter baseline from a campus tree, sight the zenith angle ($\theta$), and compute tree height. Use upward/downward light sensors to measure albedo across 4 surfaces.

🟣 3. EXPLAIN (20 min) — Mathematical Energy Balance Modeling

Model Net Solar Absorbed: $S_{\text{net}} = 1000 \times (1 - \alpha)\text{ W/m}^2$. Compare asphalt ($S_{\text{net}} \approx 920\text{ W/m}^2$, 100% sensible heat) with tree canopy ($S_{\text{net}} \approx 800\text{ W/m}^2$, 75% latent heat flux).

🟠 4. ELABORATE (15 min) — Aerial Campus Grid & Urban Heat Geography

Map campus impervious percentage vs canopy cover on a 100-cell aerial grid. Discuss historical urban modification and shade equity in South Texas neighborhoods.

🟢 5. EVALUATE (15 min) — Synthesis & Rubric Review

Students complete field sheets, verifying trigonometric calculations, energy flux balances, and environmental modification essays.

📘 Teacher Instructional Guide: Subtropical Agroecology, Nitrogen Fixation & Komorebi Grades 9–12 / APES
Standard Type Alignment Details
Biology 12.B Core 3D TEKS Analyze ecological relationships and symbiotic interactions, including mutualism in biological nitrogen fixation ($N_2 \to NH_4^+$) and multi-strata niche differentiation.
Env Science 7.A / APES Cross-Curricular Evaluate agroforestry systems, soil fertility regeneration, closed-loop nutrient cycling, and displacement of synthetic Haber-Bosch fertilizers.
Fine Arts 4.2C Advanced Art Produce original observational botanical illustrations utilizing chiaroscuro tonal shading to capture dappled light (*Komorebi*).

5E Instructional Learning Sequence

🔴 1. ENGAGE (15 min) — The Nitrogen Dilemma & Komorebi Phenomenon

Contrast industrial Haber-Bosch fertilizer runoff with ancient Tamaulipan thornscrub polycultures. Introduce Komorebi (filtered canopy light) and photoinhibition prevention.

🔵 2. EXPLORE (40 min) — Rhizobial Root Bioassay & PAR Profile

Micro-trench legume roots (Honey Mesquite, Texas Ebony). Bisect nodules to verify pink leghaemoglobin activity. Measure vertical PAR profile ($I_0 \to I_{\text{soil}}$) and soil nitrates.

🟣 3. EXPLAIN (25 min) — Nitrogenase Biochemistry & Beer-Lambert Law

Model the reduction of atmospheric $N_2 \to NH_4^+$ and apply Beer-Lambert canopy light extinction: $I = I_0 \cdot e^{-k \cdot \text{LAI}}$.

🟠 4. ELABORATE (25 min) — Chiaroscuro Botanical Drawing & Polyculture Design

Students execute an observational chiaroscuro sketch of bipinnate leaves and active nodules using 5 tonal values, then architect a 3-tier agroforestry food forest.

🟢 5. EVALUATE (15 min) — Synthesis & APES Argumentation

Students finalize their field notebooks, compiling nodule bioassays, light transmittance calculations, botanical artwork, and agroecological design arguments.
