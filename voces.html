"The Living Canopy Field Journal · Stories, Sweat, Science & Shade in South Texas"

This isn’t just an academic TEKS worksheet. It’s a love letter to the Rio Grande Valley’s dirt, heat, and trees—written with the real words, sweat, and discoveries of 3rd, 4th, and 5th-grade students across Donna, Mercedes, PSJA, McAllen, and Harlingen.

"I always thought the mesquite was just a regular tree. But when we took the laser gun and saw the asphalt was 136°F and the grass under the tree was 84°F, my jaw literally dropped. It felt like standing inside a refrigerator with leaves!"
— Sofia R. (4th Grade) Donna ISD
"When we untied the Ziploc bag, clear water dripped onto my hand. It didn’t rain today at all. The tree literally sweated for us so we could have shade at recess. I told my little brother we have to protect this tree forever."
— Mateo V. (5th Grade) Mercedes ISD
👵🏽 Lo Que Me Dijo Mi Abuelita / Valley Living Lore:
"Mijo, don’t cut the mesquite branch. In July when the sun is biting, that tree drinks the deepest water from the river underneath our feet and gives you clean breath." Long before infrared thermal sensors, Valley families understood that native South Texas canopies were the ultimate life-support system.

VALLEY STORIES

August in the RGV: Why Shade is Survival

Empirical Science meets the Lived Reality of Rio Grande Valley Schoolyards

Every kid in the Valley knows the sound of las chicharras (cicadas) singing in August when the heat hits 104°F. You run across the blacktop hopping on your toes because the rubber soles of your sneakers get soft.

"We did the math! Our tree covers 28 square meters. That means our whole table group plus the kids playing kickball can all sit in the shade without anyone getting dizzy from the sun."
— Emily & Carlos PSJA ISD
🔍 Real Field Tip from 5th Graders:
• Look for the ant trails on the north side of the trunk before sitting down!
• Keep the plastic bag tied super tight or the tree’s water breath will escape into the wind.
