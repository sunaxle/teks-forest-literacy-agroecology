## II. Master Teacher Instructional Guide (5E Cycle)

### 1. Essential Question & Phenomenon
> **Anchor Phenomenon:** Why does standing over black asphalt on a sunny midday feel like standing above an oven heating coil, whereas standing next to a light-colored concrete sidewalk or beneath a Texas Ebony canopy feels dramatically cooler—even when both receive identical solar sunlight (approx. $1000\text{ W/m}^2$)?
> 
> **Essential Question:** *How do surface albedo and tree canopy geometry control the net radiation energy flux ($R_n$) of our campus, and how did historical urban modifications shape these microclimates?*

---

### 2. Learning Objectives & Success Criteria
- **Science Objective:** Students will calculate surface albedo coefficients ($\alpha$) and model net radiation flux ($R_n = S_{\text{in}}(1-\alpha) + L_{\text{net}}$) across 4 surface types to demonstrate how vegetation converts sensible heat into latent heat.
- **Math Objective:** Students will construct and use protractor clinometers to solve right triangles ($\text{Height} = D \cdot \tan\theta + h_{\text{eye}}$) and calculate dynamic shadow vector lengths based on solar elevation angles.
- **Social Studies Objective:** Students will evaluate campus aerial maps to calculate the ratio of impervious surfaces to tree canopy and connect historical urban land-use policies with modern schoolyard thermal equity.

### 4. Safety & Outdoor Investigation Protocols
- **Solar Sighting Safety:** Emphasize sternly that students must **NEVER look directly at the Sun** through the clinometer straw. Clinometers are used to sight the **top branch of trees and building corners**, NOT the solar disk.
- **Thermal Heat Protocols:** Assign rotating team roles (Clinometer Sighter, Data Recorder, Sensor Specialist, Tape Measure Anchor) to minimize physical fatigue during hot weather. Ensure water hydration intervals every 15 minutes.

### 5. 5E Instructional Learning Sequence

#### Phase 1: ENGAGE (15 Minutes) — The Energy Balance Paradox
1. **The Energy Balance Demonstration:** Show students two identical metal cans left in the sun for 30 minutes—one painted matte black, one painted reflective silver/white. Have a student volunteer record their surface temperatures with the IR gun. Ask: *"Why did the black can absorb vastly more thermal energy when both sat in the exact same sunlight?"*
2. **Key Definitions:**
   - **Albedo ($\alpha$):** The fraction of incoming shortwave solar radiation ($S_{\text{in}}$) reflected by a surface ($0.0 = \text{total absorption / pitch black}$, $1.0 = \text{total reflection / perfect mirror}$).
   - **Sensible Heat Flux ($H$):** Thermal energy that increases air temperature (measurable by a standard thermometer).
   - **Latent Heat Flux ($\lambda E$):** Thermal energy absorbed by water during phase-change evaporation/transpiration without raising temperature.

#### Phase 2: EXPLORE (35 Minutes) — Clinometer Forestry & Albedo Audit
1. **Construct Protractor Clinometer:** Thread a 30 cm string through the center hole of a 180° protractor, tie a heavy metal nut at the bottom as a plumb bob, and tape a straight drinking straw along the 90° / straight baseline.
2. **Trigonometric Tree Height Measurement (Math 8.7B):**
   - Step back a known baseline distance ($D = 15.0\text{ meters}$) from the tagged campus tree.
   - Sight the top canopy branch through the straw. A partner reads the angle ($\theta$) where the weighted string rests.
   - Measure observer eye height ($h_{\text{eye}}$).
   - Compute tree height: $\text{Height} = (D \times \tan\theta) + h_{\text{eye}}$.
3. **Surface Albedo ($\alpha$) Measurement (Science 8.10B):**
   - Hold the light meter sensor 30 cm above a surface facing UP to measure Incident Solar Irradiance ($S_{\text{in}}$ in Lux).
   - Invert the sensor facing DOWN 30 cm above the surface to measure Reflected Solar Irradiance ($S_{\text{out}}$ in Lux).
   - Compute Albedo Coefficient: $\alpha = S_{\text{out}} \div S_{\text{in}}$.
   - Test 4 distinct surfaces: (1) Fresh Blacktop Asphalt, (2) Light Concrete Sidewalk, (3) Unshaded Turf Grass, (4) Living Tree Canopy / Shaded Mulch.

#### Phase 3: EXPLAIN (20 Minutes) — Mathematical Energy Balance Modeling
1. **Net Shortwave Energy Absorption ($S_{\text{net}}$):**
   - Given solar irradiance $S_{\text{in}} \approx 1000\text{ W/m}^2$, calculate net solar radiation absorbed: $S_{\text{net}} = 1000 \times (1 - \alpha)\text{ W/m}^2$.
   - Contrast asphalt ($S_{\text{net}} \approx 920\text{ W/m}^2$, almost entirely converted into sensible scorching heat $H$) with tree canopy ($S_{\text{net}} \approx 800\text{ W/m}^2$, where up to 75% is converted harmlessly into latent heat flux $\lambda E$ via transpiration).
2. **Shadow Vector Geometry:**
   - Use the computed tree height ($H_{\text{tree}}$) and current shadow length ($L_{\text{shadow}}$) to calculate the Solar Elevation Zenith Angle: $\theta_{\text{sun}} = \arctan(H_{\text{tree}} / L_{\text{shadow}})$.

#### Phase 4: ELABORATE (15 Minutes) — Historical Geography & Urban Tree Equity (SS 8.11A)
1. **Campus Impervious Surface Audit:** Examine an aerial satellite view of the school grounds. Divide the schoolyard into a 10x10 grid (100 cells total).
2. **Canopy Deficit Calculation:** Count how many grid cells are covered by Impervious Surfaces (asphalt, concrete, rooftops) versus Tree Canopy.
3. **Historical Analysis Discussion:** Discuss how historical municipal planning, zoning ordinances, and lack of shade investment created "urban heat islands" in specific neighborhoods, affecting community comfort, energy bills, and student academic focus.

#### Phase 5: EVALUATE (15 Minutes) — Quantitative Lab Synthesis & Rubric Review
- Students finalize their printable Field Lab Worksheet, verifying all trigonometric calculations, albedo coefficients, energy balance equations, and the Social Studies environmental modification synthesis response.

### 6. Misconception Alerts & Differentiation Scaffolds
- ❌ *Misconception:* "Albedo and temperature are the exact same thing."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Albedo is an optical property of reflectance (unitless ratio $0.0-1.0$), while temperature is a thermodynamic measure of kinetic thermal energy (°C or °F). Low albedo causes high temperature because high absorption fuels sensible heat transfer.
- ❌ *Misconception:* "Clinometer angle is directly equal to the tree height."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* The clinometer measures an angular degree of elevation ($\theta$); trigonometric tangent ratios ($\tan\theta$) and observer eye height ($h_{\text{eye}}$) are mathematically required to solve for linear height in meters.
- 🌐 **Bilingual / ELL Scaffolding:** Include visual diagrams of right triangles labeled with Spanish terms (*Cateto Opuesto / Opposite*, *Cateto Adyacente / Adjacent*, *Ángulo de Elevación / Elevation Angle*, *Flujo de Radiación Neta / Net Radiation Flux*).
- 🚀 **Advanced / GT Extension:** Apply Stefan-Boltzmann Law ($L_{\text{out}} = \epsilon \sigma T^4$) to calculate emitted longwave thermal radiation in $\text{W/m}^2$ using IR surface temperatures converted to Kelvin ($K = ^\circ\text{C} + 273.15$).
