## II. Master Teacher Instructional Guide (5E Cycle)

### 1. Essential Question & Phenomenon
> **Anchor Phenomenon:** When sitting quietly beneath the dappled shade (*sombra moteada*) of a Honey Mesquite (*Mezquite Dulce*) or Texas Ebony (*Ébano*) on a warm South Texas afternoon, how do our five senses detect the living energy flow, cooling moisture, and peaceful microclimate that cannot be felt on open concrete?
> 
> **Essential Question:** *How can we use scientific sensory observation, botanical sketching, and bilingual poetic language to capture the living heartbeat of our campus microforest?*

---

### 2. Learning Objectives & Success Criteria
- **ELAR Objective:** Students will conduct a 7-minute silent "Sit Spot" protocol, logging at least 6 descriptive sensory observations (sight, sound, touch, smell, thermal sensation) in bilingual English/Spanish, and compose a structured nature poem (Haiku or Cinquain).
- **Science Objective:** Students will record the microclimate temperature delta ($\Delta T$) between open blacktop and tree canopy using an infrared thermometer in both Metric (°C) and Imperial (°F) units.
- **Fine Arts Objective:** Students will produce an accurate scientific botanical sketch of a leaf cluster and bark section with labeled anatomical structures and a scale bar in centimeters/inches ($\text{cm / in}$).
- **Health Objective:** Students will self-assess mental calmness and physical comfort before and after the 15-minute nature immersion.

### 5. 5E Instructional Learning Sequence

#### Phase 1: ENGAGE (10 Minutes) — The Sensory Tuning Fork
1. **Sensory Reset:** Gather students at the outdoor transition door. Instruct students to take one deep breath and close their eyes for 30 seconds. Ask: *"What do your ears notice right now? What does your skin feel?"*
2. **Introduce the "Sit Spot" (*Lugar de Asiento*) Protocol:** Explain that a Sit Spot is an individual quiet zone under a tree where the investigator becomes completely still—like a resting Texas Spiny Lizard—allowing nature to return to its natural rhythm.
3. **Bilingual Word Bank Briefing:** Review primary terms: *Sombra Moteada* (Dappled Shade / Komorebi), *Corteza* (Bark), *Hojas Compuestas* (Compound Leaves), *Viento del Sur* (Southern Breeze).
4. **Walk Out:** Walk silently in single file to the campus microforest grove.

#### Phase 2 & 3: EXPLORE & EXPLAIN (25 Minutes) — 3 Field Investigation Stations

##### Station 1: Silent Sit Spot Sensory Inventory (`STA-JOURNAL-SIT-SPOT` · 8 Min)
- Students spread out to individual designated trees (minimum 2 meters / 6 feet apart).
- **Rule of Absolute Silence (7 Minutes):** Students sit on foam pads and record observations in the 5-Sense Matrix:
  - **Sight (*Vista*):** Light filtering through leaflets, fluttering wings, ants on bark.
  - **Sound (*Sonido*):** Rustling leaves, cicada hum, bird chirps, wind gust.
  - **Touch (*Tacto*):** Roughness of bark, cool shaded soil, warmth of sunbeam.
  - **Smell (*Olfato*):** Earthy humus, sweet mesquite blossoms, dry dust.
  - **Thermal Delta (*Temperatura*):** Measure ground temperature under tree vs sunlit sidewalk in °C and °F using the IR thermometer.

##### Station 2: Botanical Macro-Sketching & Scale (`STA-BOTANICAL-SKETCH` · 9 Min)
- Using the 10x hand lens, students closely inspect a living branch or fallen leaf cluster.
- In the boxed Field Sketch workspace, students draw:
  1. Leaf Margin & Venation Network (*Red de nervaduras*)
  2. Bark Surface Texture Fissures (*Grietas de la corteza*)
- Students add a dual-unit scale bar ($2\text{ cm} \approx 0.8\text{ in}$) and anatomical labels (*Petiolo, Folíolo, Corteza*).

##### Station 3: Bilingual Poetry Drafting (`STA-BILINGUAL-POETRY` · 8 Min)
- Students select one poetic structure from their clipboard worksheet:
  - **Option A: Microforest Haiku (5-7-5 Syllables)**  
    *Example:*  
    *Green mesquite leaves sway,* (5)  
    *Cooling the hot Texas sun,* (7)  
    *Shadows dance in peace.* (5)  
  - **Option B: Bilingual Tree Cinquain (2-4-6-8-2 Words/Syllables)**  
    *Tree / Árbol*  
    *Deep roots, rough bark*  
    *Drinking sun, exhaling breeze*  
    *Green sanctuary in the heat*  
    *Living refuge.*  

#### Phase 4: ELABORATE (5 Minutes) — Paired Peer Reading & Thermal Synthesis
- Students pair up with their nearest field partner and perform a whisper-reading of their nature poem.
- Partners calculate their shared temperature delta: $\Delta T = T_{\text{blacktop}} - T_{\text{canopy}}$.

#### Phase 5: EVALUATE (5 Minutes) — Reflection & Return Protocol
- Complete the 2-question formative check on the worksheet.
- Pack clipboards, ensure zero litter left behind (Leave No Trace / *No Deje Rastro*), and transition indoors.

## III. Misconception Alerts & Differentiation Scaffolds

- ❌ *Misconception:* "Nature journaling is just drawing pictures; it's not real science."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Observational logging is the foundation of field biology, forestry, and ecology. Historic naturalists like Alexander von Humboldt and Charles Darwin made groundbreaking scientific discoveries through descriptive sensory field notebooks and scaled illustrations.
- ❌ *Misconception:* "Poetry doesn't belong in outdoor science."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Scientific communication requires precise descriptive adjectives and synthesis. Translating quantitative temperature data and sensory inputs into structured poetry strengthens cross-hemispheric neural connections (ELAR 5.11A + Science 5.12B).
- 💡 *Scaffolding for Emerging Bilinguals:* Provide sentence frames on the clipboard (*"Under the [Mezquite/Ebony], I hear the [sonido] of..." / "Beneath the canopy, the temperature is ____°C cooler than the asphalt..."*).
