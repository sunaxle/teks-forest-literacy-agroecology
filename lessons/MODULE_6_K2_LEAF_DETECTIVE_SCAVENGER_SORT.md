## II. Master Teacher Instructional Guide (5E Cycle)

### 1. Essential Question & Phenomenon
> **Anchor Phenomenon:** Look down at the leaves on the ground! Why are some leaves broad and smooth like a dinner plate, while Honey Mesquite leaves are made of dozens of tiny green feather-like leaflets?
> 
> **Essential Question:** *How do leaf shapes, sizes, and margins help us identify South Texas trees and discover how they survive in hot, dry weather?*

---

### 2. Learning Objectives & Success Criteria
- **Science Objective:** Students will safely collect 5 fallen leaves from the ground (following the "Never Pick Living Leaves" stewardship rule) and classify them by margin (smooth vs. toothed) and type (simple vs. compound).
- **Math Objective:** Students will sort their leaves onto a 2-circle Venn/sorting mat, measure length in centimeters and inches ($\text{cm / in}$), and tally the count.
- **ELAR/Art Objective:** Students will trace a leaf perimeter, sketch vein pathways, and write two bilingual descriptive words on their clipboard worksheet.

### 5. 5E Instructional Learning Sequence

#### Phase 1: ENGAGE (10 Minutes) — The Golden Rule of Forest Detectives
1. **The Stewardship Oath (Leave No Trace):** Gather students. Teach the Detective Rule:  
   *"A true Leaf Detective gathers leaves that have fallen to the ground like natural gifts; we NEVER pull or rip living leaves from the branches!"*
2. **Mystery Leaf Showcase:** Show two contrasting leaves:
   - A broad, velvety Mexican Olive (*Anacahuita*) leaf.
   - A delicate bipinnate Honey Mesquite (*Mezquite Dulce*) compound twig.
   - Ask: *"Which leaf do you think loses less water on a 100°F summer afternoon?"*
3. **Walk Out:** Walk with collection trays to the campus microforest trees.

#### Phase 2 & 3: EXPLORE & EXPLAIN (25 Minutes) — 2 Field Stations

##### Station 1: Ground Leaf Scavenger Collection (`STA-LEAF-COLLECTION` · 12 Min)
- Pairs search the mulch and ground beneath at least 2 different tree species.
- Each team finds **5 different fallen leaves**:
  - Target 1: A leaf with a smooth edge (*Margen liso*).
  - Target 2: A leaf with tiny teeth/fissures (*Margen aserrado*).
  - Target 3: A compound twig with tiny leaflets (*Folíolos de mezquite/ébano*).
  - Target 4: The longest leaf they can find.
  - Target 5: The smallest leaflet they can find.

##### Station 2: Sorting Mat & Hand-Lens Inspection (`STA-LEAF-SORT-MAT` · 13 Min)
- **Sorting on the Mat (Math 1.8A):** Place collection tray on the outdoor sorting mat:
  - *Left Zone:* Smooth Edges (*Bordes Lisos*)
  - *Right Zone:* Toothed / Bumpy Edges (*Bordes Aserrados / Rugosos*)
- **Dual-Unit Leaf Measurement:** Use the clear ruler to measure:
  - Longest leaf length: _____ $\text{cm}$ / _____ $\text{inches}$
  - Smallest leaflet length: _____ $\text{cm}$ / _____ $\text{inches}$
- **Hand-Lens Venation Trace (Art 1.1B):** Use the 10x hand lens to inspect how leaf veins branch out like rivers. Place one leaf beneath the worksheet box and trace its outline with a pencil.

#### Phase 4: ELABORATE (5 Minutes) — The Desert Heat Secret (Science 2.13A)
- Explain why Honey Mesquite and Texas Ebony have compound leaves with dozens of tiny leaflets:
  - *Big flat leaves catch a lot of sun, but lose lots of water through evaporation.*
  - *Tiny leaflets let hot wind blow right through and keep precious water inside the tree!*

#### Phase 5: EVALUATE (5 Minutes) — Pictograph Tally & Debrief
- Students complete the leaf count tally on their clipboard worksheet.
- Return all collected leaves back to the mulch bed to nourish the soil.

## III. Misconception Alerts & Differentiation Scaffolds

- ❌ *Misconception:* "A compound mesquite twig with 20 tiny leaflets is 20 separate leaves."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* In compound leaves, the entire stem with all its tiny leaflets is technically **one single leaf** attached at a bud.
- ❌ *Misconception:* "Brown fallen leaves are useless trash."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Fallen leaves decompose into organic mulch (*hojarasca*), creating rich soil nutrients and sheltering ground organisms.
- 💡 *Scaffolding for K-1 Learners:* Provide physical sorting bowls labeled with color-coded tactile textures (smooth sticker vs rough sandpaper).
