## Executive Curriculum Architecture Overview

The **SombraTEKS Master Curriculum** provides a rigorous, 52-module vertical progression designed specifically for Texas school districts. Every grade level from **Kindergarten through Grade 12** completes one comprehensive 45-minute experiential outdoor lab per academic quarter across four interconnected seasonal themes:

| Quarter | Theme | Focus Area | Recurring Cross-Cutting Concept (RTC) | Scientific & Engineering Practice (SEP) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Quarter 1 (Fall)** | **PLT Theme 1: What is a Forest?** | Canopy Physics & Microclimate Thermodynamics | Patterns & Energy/Matter Flux | Collecting, Recording & Analyzing Observational Data |
| **Quarter 2 (Winter)** | **PLT Theme 3: How We Relate to Forests** | Biomass Allometry, Dendrology & Geometry | Scale, Proportion, and Quantity | Using Mathematics & Computational Thinking |
| **Quarter 3 (Spring)** | **PLT Theme 2: Why Do Forests Matter?** | Subtropical Agroecology, Soils & Mycorrhizae | Systems & System Models | Developing & Using Models / Investigating Interactions |
| **Quarter 4 (Late Spring)** | **PLT Theme 4: What is the Future?** | Community Heat Equity, Policy & Cultural Ecology | Stability & Change / Cause & Effect | Constructing Explanations & Designing Solutions |

## Module 01: LAB-K-Q1 — Tree Senses & Living Shade Exploration
**Grade Band:** K-2 (Kindergarten) | **Quarter:** Q1 (Fall Canopy Physics & Microclimate Thermodynamics)  
**Primary Subject:** CROSS_DISCIPLINARY | **Mastery Points:** 100 pts  
**PLT Framework Theme:** Theme 1 — What is a Forest? (Canopy & Microclimate Physics)  

> ### 🎯 TEKS & State Standards Alignment
> - **Aligned TEKS Codes:** `Science K.12A, Science K.12B, Math K.2A, ELAR K.2A, Health K.1A, Art K.1A`
> - **Science RTC (Cross-Cutting Concept):** Patterns & Energy/Matter Flux
> - **Science SEP (Practice):** Collecting, Recording, and Analyzing Observational Data

### 1. Guiding Phenomenon & RGV Context
Under peak South Texas sunlight, ambient schoolyard temperatures can swing by over 25°F between exposed concrete and native canopy cover (*Prosopis glandulosa*, *Ebenopsis ebano*, *Quercus virginiana*). Students observe how physical and biological mechanisms convert solar radiation into cooling living infrastructure.

### 2. Targeted Learning Objectives
- Use all five senses to observe bark texture, leaf rustling sounds, and shade cooling.
- Compare the air temperature standing in direct sunlight versus standing under a Honey Mesquite tree canopy.
- Draw and describe three living features of a campus shade tree.

### 3. Key Bilingual Vocabulary
*Árbol (Tree), Sombra (Shade), Hoja (Leaf), Raíz (Root), Tronco (Trunk), Corteza (Bark), Calor (Heat)*

### 4. 45-Minute Outdoor Lab Protocol: *Five-Sense Campus Tree Hugger Tour*
- **Total Duration:** 30 Minutes
- **Required Equipment & Field Tools:**
  - Magnifying hand lens
  - Sensory picture checklists
  - Crayons & drawing clipboards

#### Step-by-Step Field Investigation Timetable:
| Phase | Time Window | Teacher & Student Actions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phase 1: Field Hook & Calibration** | 0 – 10 min | Review safety guardrails, distribute field gear, calibrate sensors/meters, and establish campus survey stations. |
| **Phase 2: Empirical Data Collection** | 10 – 30 min | **Students partner up to touch smooth vs rough bark, listen to breeze through leaves, feel the ground shade delta, and sketch what they see.** Students record observations in duplicate across designated zones. |
| **Phase 3: Mathematical Modeling & Analysis** | 30 – 40 min | Calculate quantitative parameters (means, ratios, delta T, biomass, infiltration rates) and complete field data logs. |
| **Phase 4: Synthesis & Formative Exit Check** | 40 – 45 min | Reconvene in outdoor shade amphitheater, discuss ecological significance, and complete independent formative check. |

### 5. Formative Assessment & STAAR-Format Item
**Item Prompt:** How does the ground feel when you sit underneath a big shady tree compared to hot asphalt?
- **[A]** Much hotter
- **[B]** Noticeably cooler and shaded *(Correct)*
- **[C]** Exactly the same
- **[D]** Wet like a swimming pool

**Diagnostic Rationale:** Tree leaves block intense solar rays and release moisture through transpiration, creating cool shade.

### 6. Safety & Environmental Stewardship
- Ensure all students remain hydrated and wear sun protection during outdoor fieldwork.
- Maintain strict respect for campus flora: do not strip live bark or snap living branches; utilize fallen specimens and non-destructive measurement tools.
