## II. Master Teacher Instructional Guide (5E Cycle)

### 1. Essential Question & Phenomenon
> **Anchor Phenomenon:** Close your eyes and run your fingers along the trunk of a Honey Mesquite, then touch a young Cedar Elm. Every tree species has its own unique tactile "fingerprint" carved into its bark! How can an artist capture this hidden 3D pattern on a flat sheet of paper?
> 
> **Essential Question:** *How can we use the art technique of friction rubbing (*frotado / frottage*) to capture and compare the living texture fingerprints of South Texas trees?*

---

### 2. Learning Objectives & Success Criteria
- **Fine Arts Objective:** Students will create 2 distinct, high-contrast bark friction rubbings (*frotados*) capturing the raised surface topography of native campus trees.
- **Science Objective:** Students will explain how the thickness and rough ridges of bark protect the tree trunk from extreme sun radiation and pests.
- **Math Objective:** Students will measure the width of bark grooves in centimeters and inches ($\text{cm / in}$) and compare wide vs. narrow furrow spacing.
- **ELAR Objective:** Students will write 3 bilingual texture words beneath their artwork.

### 5. 5E Instructional Learning Sequence

#### Phase 1: ENGAGE (10 Minutes) — The Mystery Coin & Bark Rubbing Demo
1. **The Mystery Coin Demonstration:** Place a quarter under a sheet of white paper. Rub the side of an unwrapped crayon across the paper to reveal the hidden embossed eagle. Explain:  
   *"This printmaking technique is called **Frottage** (*frotado*). Today, our campus trees will be our printing plates!"*
2. **Gentle Tree Stewardship Rule:** Emphasize that we use light painter's tape and gentle crayon pressure so we do not pull or damage living tree bark.
3. **Walk Out:** Carry clipboards and unwrapped crayons to the campus tree grove.

#### Phase 2 & 3: EXPLORE & EXPLAIN (25 Minutes) — 2 Field Stations

##### Station 1: Tree Trunk Rubbing Studio (`STA-BARK-STUDIO` · 13 Min)
- Students choose two contrasting native trees (e.g., Honey Mesquite with checkered grooves vs. Cedar Elm or Mexican Olive).
- **Frottage Technique:**
  1. Position the worksheet's "Bark Print 1" box flat against the tree trunk at chest height.
  2. Use painter's tape to hold the edges gently.
  3. Rub the flat belly of the unwrapped wax crayon sideways across the paper.
  4. Watch the raised bark ridges transfer onto the paper in high contrast!
- Repeat for "Bark Print 2" on the second tree species.
- **Math Groove Measurement (Math 1.7A):** Use the clear ruler to measure the distance between two major bark ridges ($1\text{ cm} \approx 0.4\text{ in}$).

##### Station 2: Fallen Leaf Relief & Venation Print (`STA-LEAF-RELIEF` · 12 Min)
- Students pick up 1 sturdy fallen leaf from the ground.
- Place the leaf **vein-side UP** beneath the "Leaf Relief" box on the worksheet.
- Hold the paper firmly and rub the side of a green or terracotta crayon over the leaf.
- Observe how the xylem/phloem veins act as raised skeletal ridges that transfer onto the page.

#### Phase 4: ELABORATE (5 Minutes) — Pop-Up Outdoor Art Gallery
- Students clip their finished worksheets onto a communal clothesline or lay them along a shaded bench.
- Conduct a 3-minute silent "Gallery Walk" (*Paseo de Galería*), admiring the diversity of bark textures.

#### Phase 5: EVALUATE (5 Minutes) — Formative Check & Clean Up
- Carefully peel off painter's tape and ensure zero trash left behind.
- Complete the 2-question formative check and return indoors.

## III. Misconception Alerts & Differentiation Scaffolds

- ❌ *Misconception:* "You use the sharp tip of the crayon like a pencil to color the tree."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Using the tip punches through paper and misses the texture. Rubbing with the **flat long side of the crayon cylinder** captures the raised 3D surface topography evenly.
- ❌ *Misconception:* "All tree bark is the same brown color and roughness."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Bark varies widely in color (gray, amber, reddish-brown), thickness, and geometry (checkered blocks, long vertical ribbons, smooth lenticels).
- 💡 *Scaffolding for Fine-Motor Support:* Provide oversized chunky preschool wax blocks for students developing grip strength.
