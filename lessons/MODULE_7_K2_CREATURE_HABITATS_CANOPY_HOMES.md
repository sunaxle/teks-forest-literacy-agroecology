## II. Master Teacher Instructional Guide (5E Cycle)

### 1. Essential Question & Phenomenon
> **Anchor Phenomenon:** Look closely at a campus tree. You are looking at a living multi-story apartment building! Who lives on the top penthouse branches, who crawls along the wooden hallways of the trunk, and who burrows in the basement soil beneath the roots?
> 
> **Essential Question:** *How does a single campus tree provide food, water, and shelter (*hábitat y refugio*) for diverse South Texas wildlife?*

---

### 2. Learning Objectives & Success Criteria
- **Science Objective:** Students will identify at least 3 living organisms that rely on campus trees for survival and distinguish between living (*biótico*) and non-living (*abiótico*) components of a tree habitat.
- **Math Objective:** Students will record wildlife observation tallies across 3 canopy vertical zones and summarize counts in a bar/picture chart.
- **Social Studies/ELAR Objective:** Students will describe how native South Texas wildlife (such as Green Jays and Anole lizards) depend on native thornscrub tree canopies.
- **Fine Arts Objective:** Students will illustrate a "Tree Hotel" showing animal residents in their respective tree layers.

### 5. 5E Instructional Learning Sequence

#### Phase 1: ENGAGE (10 Minutes) — The "Tree Hotel" Concept
1. **The Tree Hotel Analogy:** Gather students in the courtyard. Ask:  
   *"If a Honey Mesquite or Texas Ebony is a 3-story hotel, who lives in the penthouse roof (the canopy)? Who lives in the middle rooms (the trunk and branches)? Who lives in the basement (the root soil)?"*
2. **Quiet Wildlife Protocol:** Emphasize that wild creatures have sensitive ears. To spot wildlife, investigators must move like gentle shadows and use whisper voices.
3. **Walk to Microforest:** Walk with binoculars to the canopy observation area.

#### Phase 2 & 3: EXPLORE & EXPLAIN (25 Minutes) — 2 Field Stations

##### Station 1: Silent Canopy Binocular Watch (`STA-CANOPY-WATCH` · 13 Min)
- Students focus binoculars on the high branches and upper crown ($3\text{ to }6\text{ meters} / 10\text{ to }20\text{ feet}$ above ground).
- **Canopy Wildlife Scan (7 Minutes):** Look for:
  - Flying visitors: Green Jay (*Chara Verde*), Northern Mockingbird, Honeybees (*Abejas*), Butterflies (*Mariposas*).
  - Nesting signs: Twig nests (*nidos*), hanging leaves, web structures.
- Students record tallies in the **Canopy Zone** table.

##### Station 2: Trunk Bark & Mulch Micro-Habitat Search (`STA-BARK-SOIL-MICRO` · 12 Min)
- Pairs kneel near the trunk base ($0\text{ to }1.5\text{ meters} / 0\text{ to }5\text{ feet}$ height) and search bark furrows and surrounding soil mulch.
- **Hand-Lens Micro-Search:** Look for:
  - Trunk residents: Green Anole lizards (*Lagartijas*), ants (*Hormigas*), cicada shells (*Chicharras*).
  - Soil & Mulch decomposers: Beetles (*Escarabajos*), pill bugs (*Cochinillas*), earthworms (*Lombrices*).
- Students record tallies in the **Trunk & Soil Zone** table.

#### Phase 4: ELABORATE (5 Minutes) — Biotic vs. Abiotic Tree Connections
- Discuss how living creatures (*biótico*) depend on non-living parts (*abiótico*):
  - *Birds drink abiotic rainwater droplets off leaves.*
  - *Lizards warm their bodies in abiotic sunlight resting on biotic tree bark.*
  - *Trees provide biotic shade, cooling the ground by up to 15°F / 8°C!*

#### Phase 5: EVALUATE (5 Minutes) — Wildlife Tally Chart & Check
- Students summarize their wildlife counts and complete the 2-question formative check.
- Pack binoculars safely and transition indoors.

## III. Misconception Alerts & Differentiation Scaffolds

- ❌ *Misconception:* "If we don't see a bird right away, the tree has no wildlife."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Even when animals are hiding, trees show abundant **indirect evidence of life** (spiderwebs, chewed leaves, bird droppings, hollow cavities, insect cocoons).
- ❌ *Misconception:* "Insects only hurt the tree."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Native pollinators fertilize tree flowers, and decomposers break down fallen debris into fertile organic soil that feeds the tree.
- 💡 *Scaffolding for K-1 Students:* Provide picture cards of local wildlife to match against outdoor observations.
