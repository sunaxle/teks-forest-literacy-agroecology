## II. Master Teacher Instructional Guide (5E Cycle)

### 1. Essential Question & Phenomenon
> **Anchor Phenomenon:** Thousands of years before supermarkets, grocery stores, and water pipes existed in the Rio Grande Valley, indigenous Coahuiltecan families thrived through scorching, bone-dry summer droughts. They called the thorny Honey Mesquite their sacred "Tree of Life." How did indigenous peoples harvest, mill, and utilize every single part of this drought-hardy tree for sweet food, refreshing drinks, healing medicine, building tools, and deep cooling shade?
> 
> **Essential Question:** *How did South Texas indigenous peoples utilize the anatomical adaptations of the Honey Mesquite tree to sustain thriving communities in an arid environment?*

---

### 2. Learning Objectives & Success Criteria
- **Social Studies Objective:** Students will describe 4 distinct indigenous uses of the Honey Mesquite (nutrition/flour, building poles, cordage fibers, eye medicine/gum) and explain how Coahuiltecan communities adapted to seasonal droughts.
- **Science Objective:** Students will examine the xerophytic adaptations of mesquite: bipinnately compound leaflets, photosynthetic green bark, and 50-foot taproots that reach deep underground water tables.
- **Math Objective:** Students will measure pod lengths ($\text{cm / in}$), weigh pod samples ($\text{g / oz}$), and calculate the percentage of edible sweet flour yield.
- **Health Objective:** Students will compare natural mesquite flour (*pinole*) to refined sugar, explaining why it was a healthy, sustained energy source for indigenous hunters and gatherers.

### 5. 5E Instructional Learning Sequence

#### Phase 1: ENGAGE (10 Minutes) — The Sweet Pod Mystery Hook
1. **The Pechita Snap Test:** Gather students beneath a campus Honey Mesquite (*Prosopis glandulosa*). Hand each student a clean, dry, golden pod:
   - *"Snap it in half near your ear! Hear that crisp crunch? Smells like sweet caramel and graham crackers! This crunchy pod was the chocolate, bread, and vitamins of the Rio Grande Valley for over 10,000 years."*
2. **Bilingual Vocabulary Chant:** Call-and-response:
   - *Honey Mesquite = Mezquite Dulce!*
   - *Pechita = Edible ripe bean pod!*
   - *Metate = Traditional stone grinding slab!*
   - *Pinole = Sweet ground mesquite flour meal!*
3. **Walk to Microforest Stations:** Assign teams to their starting rotation stations.

#### Phase 2 & 3: EXPLORE & EXPLAIN (25 Minutes) — 3 Rotational Field Stations

##### Station 1: Pechita Pod Morphology & Snap Survey (`STA-PECHITA-POD-SURVEY` · 8 Min)
- **Pod Physical Dimensions (Math 5.4H):**
  - Select 3 intact pods. Measure total length: _______ $\text{cm}$ / _______ $\text{inches}$.
  - Weigh the 3-pod bundle: _______ $\text{grams (g)}$ / _______ $\text{ounces (oz)}$.
- **Anatomy & Hand-Lens Inspection (Science 5.12B):**
  - Count internal hard seeds per pod (approx. 12–20 seeds per pod).
  - Inspect the spongy, sugary middle layer (**mesocarp** / *pulpa dulce*).
  - Explain that the sweet pulp contains 30% natural sugars (fructose and sucrose) along with 13% protein and soluble dietary fiber.

##### Station 2: Traditional Metate Stone Milling Demo (`STA-METATE-MILLING-DEMO` · 8 Min)
- **Crushing & Grinding:** Place 2 snapped pods into the stone mortar or on the metate. Students take turns using the pestle to crush the dried brittle pods with firm rocking motions.
- **Separation & Sifting:** Observe how the dry pulp turns into a fine golden flour (*harina de mezquite*), while the rock-hard seed casings remain intact.
- **Nutrition & Health Comparison (Health 5.3A):**
  - Unlike modern white flour which causes sudden blood sugar spikes, mesquite flour contains complex galactomannan fibers that release energy slowly over many hours of hunting and gathering.
  - Traditional preparation: mixed with cool well water to make *Atole de Mezquite* or molded into sun-dried travel cakes (*mezquitamal*).

##### Station 3: Deep Taproot & Arid Drought Adaptation Audit (`STA-TAPROOT-ADAPTATION-AUDIT` · 8 Min)
- **The 50-Foot Taproot Wonder (Science 4.12B / 5.12B):**
  - Mesquite trees grow a specialized primary taproot that can drill straight down $15\text{ to }50\text{ meters}$ ($50\text{ to }160\text{ feet}$) into underground aquifers!
  - Lateral surface roots spread out $10\text{ to }15\text{ meters}$ ($33\text{ to }50\text{ feet}$) around the tree to catch rare flash desert rains.
- **Soil Moisture & Temperature Verification:**
  - Measure surface temperature under mesquite canopy ($29^\circ\text{C} / 84^\circ\text{F}$) vs bare exposed dirt ($50^\circ\text{C} / 122^\circ\text{F}$).
  - Inspect the nitrogen-fixing leaf mulch beneath the tree that enriches poor alkaline clay soils naturally without synthetic fertilizers.

#### Phase 4: ELABORATE (5 Minutes) — Whole-Tree Indigenous Utility Matrix
- In the field notebook, review the 5 parts of the Mesquite Tree used by Coahuiltecans:
  1. *Pods (Pechitas):* Staple food flour, sweet beverage (*atole*), travel trail bars.
  2. *Hard Wood (Madera):* Digging sticks, bows, shelter ramada posts, slow-burning firewood.
  3. *Sap / Gum (Goma de Mezquite):* Black basket dye, waterproof adhesive glue, throat soothing tea.
  4. *Inner Bark (Corteza Interior):* Twisted into tough cordage ropes, fishing nets, and woven sandals.
  5. *Leaves (Hojas):* Crushed into a soothing poultice for irritated eyes and sunburns.

#### Phase 5: EVALUATE (5 Minutes) — Ethnobotany Formative Check & Wrap-Up
- Students complete the 2-question historical thinking check.
- Clean stone mortars and return clipboards.

## III. Misconception Alerts & Differentiation Scaffolds

- ❌ *Misconception:* "Mesquite trees are invasive weeds with no value."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Honey Mesquite is a keystone native species in South Texas. It was the central life-support tree for indigenous civilizations for millennia and plays a vital role in fixing nitrogen and feeding wildlife.
- ❌ *Misconception:* "Indigenous people destroyed trees when harvesting food."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Traditional ecological stewardship involved harvesting only fallen or ripe pods in late summer without damaging the branches, ensuring healthy harvests for future generations.
- 💡 *Scaffolding for Visual Learners:* Provide physical touch samples of raw pods, crushed meal, sifted flour, and hardened amber mesquite gum.
- 🚀 *Advanced Extension (GT):* Research modern culinary agroforestry: how Rio Grande Valley chef cooperatives and bakeries are re-introducing mesquite flour into gluten-free breads and regional cuisine.
