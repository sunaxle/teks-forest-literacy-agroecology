## II. Master Teacher Instructional Guide (5E Cycle)

### 1. Essential Question & Phenomenon
> **Anchor Phenomenon:** On a hot South Texas day, the open asphalt playground reaches an unbearable $130^\circ\text{F} / 54.4^\circ\text{C}$, but a single mature Live Oak or Cedar Elm casts a massive circular umbrella of cool dark shade. How can we use our own footsteps as walking rulers and circular geometry ($A = \pi r^2$) to calculate exactly how many square meters and square feet of cool playground shelter our campus trees provide?
> 
> **Essential Question:** *How can we use mathematical pacing and geometry to calculate the cooling shade footprint and student capacity of campus trees?*

---

### 2. Learning Objectives & Success Criteria
- **Math Objective:** Students will calibrate their individual step pace factor ($\text{m/step}$ and $\text{ft/step}$), measure 4 cardinal shadow radii ($r_{\text{North}}, r_{\text{South}}, r_{\text{East}}, r_{\text{West}}$), compute average radius ($r_{\text{avg}}$), and calculate circular shade area ($A = 3.14 \times r^2$) in both metric ($\text{m}^2$) and imperial ($\text{sq ft}$) units.
- **Science Objective:** Students will measure ground surface temperature deltas between unshaded asphalt and shaded tree canopy using infrared thermometers.
- **Health Objective:** Students will divide total shade area by $1.5\text{ m}^2$ ($16.1\text{ sq ft}$) to determine the maximum number of students who can safely find thermal refuge during peak sun recess.
- **ELAR/Social Studies Objective:** Students will draft a data-driven proposal recommending new tree planting sites for unshaded play zones.

### 5. 5E Instructional Learning Sequence

#### Phase 1: ENGAGE (10 Minutes) — The "Living Tape Measure" Hook
1. **The Human Ruler Challenge:** Gather students along a 10-meter sidewalk line. Ask:
   - *"If you are lost in the woods without a tape measure, how can your two feet become a precise scientific measuring tool?"*
2. **Bilingual Vocabulary Chant:** Call-and-response:
   - *Radius = Radio (Trunk to shadow edge!)*
   - *Diameter = Diámetro (Edge to edge through center!)*
   - *Circumference = Circunferencia (Distance all the way around!)*
   - *Area = Área (The total flat ground covered!)*
3. **Walk to Microforest:** Assign teams to numbered target trees.

#### Phase 2 & 3: EXPLORE & EXPLAIN (25 Minutes) — 3 Rotational Field Stations

##### Station 1: Personal Step Pace Calibration Transect (`STA-PACE-CALIBRATION` · 8 Min)
- **10-Step Walk Protocol:** Lay out the 10-meter ($32.8\text{ ft}$) tape measure. Each student walks 10 natural, relaxed walking steps along the tape line.
- **Pace Factor Calculation (Math 4.8C & 5.4H):**
  - Total Distance for 10 steps = _______ $\text{meters (m)}$ / _______ $\text{feet (ft)}$
  - Metric Pace Factor = $\text{Distance} \div 10 = \text{______ m/step}$ (e.g., $0.65\text{ m/step}$)
  - Imperial Pace Factor = $\text{Distance} \div 10 = \text{______ ft/step}$ (e.g., $2.13\text{ ft/step}$)
- Record personal pace factor in the worksheet header for use at subsequent stations.

##### Station 2: 4-Direction Shadow Radius Pacing (`STA-SHADOW-RADIUS-PACE` · 8 Min)
- **Shadow Edge Marking:** Locate the central tree trunk. Using the compass, walk out to the outermost shaded boundary in 4 cardinal directions and place a chalk dot:
  - *North Radius ($r_1$):* _______ steps $\times$ pace factor = _______ $\text{m}$ (_______ $\text{ft}$)
  - *South Radius ($r_2$):* _______ steps $\times$ pace factor = _______ $\text{m}$ (_______ $\text{ft}$)
  - *East Radius ($r_3$):* _______ steps $\times$ pace factor = _______ $\text{m}$ (_______ $\text{ft}$)
  - *West Radius ($r_4$):* _______ steps $\times$ pace factor = _______ $\text{m}$ (_______ $\text{ft}$)
- **Average Radius ($r_{\text{avg}}$):**
  $$r_{\text{avg}} = \frac{r_1 + r_2 + r_3 + r_4}{4} = \text{_______ meters (_______ feet)}$$

##### Station 3: Shade Area & Recess Capacity Calculator (`STA-SHADE-AREA-CAPACITY` · 8 Min)
- **Circular Area Computation ($A = 3.14 \times r^2$):**
  - Step A: Multiply radius by itself: $(r_{\text{avg}})^2 = \text{_______} \times \text{_______} = \text{_______ } \text{m}^2$ ($\text{sq ft}$)
  - Step B: Multiply by $\pi \approx 3.14$: $\text{Total Shade Area} = 3.14 \times (r_{\text{avg}})^2 = \text{_______ } \text{m}^2$ ($\text{_______ sq ft}$)
- **Thermal Refuge Capacity (Health 5.3A):**
  - Each elementary student requires approximately $1.5\text{ m}^2$ ($16.1\text{ sq ft}$) of personal play/rest space.
  - Safe Student Capacity = $\text{Total Area } (\text{m}^2) \div 1.5\text{ m}^2 = \text{_______ students!}$
- **Microclimate Surface Verification (Science 5.12B):**
  - Log temperature under the tree ($28^\circ\text{C} / 82^\circ\text{F}$) vs unshaded asphalt ($52^\circ\text{C} / 125^\circ\text{F}$).

#### Phase 4: ELABORATE (5 Minutes) — Whole-Campus Shade Extrapolation
- If our campus has 15 mature shade trees with this average size, calculate the total schoolyard shade refuge:
  $$\text{Whole Campus Shade} = 15 \times \text{Single Tree Area} = \text{_______ } \text{m}^2 \text{ (_______ sq ft)}$$
- Discuss: *"How does planting 10 more trees make recess safer for everyone?"*

#### Phase 5: EVALUATE (5 Minutes) — Formative Math Check & Wrap-Up
- Students complete the 2-question geometric check and ensure all calculations are boxed.
- Gather equipment and return indoors.

## III. Misconception Alerts & Differentiation Scaffolds

- ❌ *Misconception:* "Tree shadows are always perfect circles."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Solar angles, branch asymmetry, and slope cause shadows to be irregular ovals; averaging 4 cardinal radii produces the best circular geometric approximation.
- ❌ *Misconception:* "Squaring a number means multiplying it by 2 ($r^2 = 2r$)."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Squaring means multiplying the number by itself ($r \times r$). For example, $4^2 = 4 \times 4 = 16$, not 8!
- 💡 *Scaffolding for Struggling Learners:* Provide a step-by-step boxed calculation grid and pre-calculated $\pi$ multiplication reference table.
- 🚀 *Advanced Extension (GT):* Calculate the perimeter (circumference $C = 2\pi r$) of the shadow and determine the linear distance needed to run once around the shade circle.
