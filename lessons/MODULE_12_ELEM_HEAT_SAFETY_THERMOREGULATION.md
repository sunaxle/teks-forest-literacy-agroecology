## II. Master Teacher Instructional Guide (5E Cycle)

### 1. Essential Question & Phenomenon
> **Anchor Phenomenon:** On an August afternoon in the Rio Grande Valley, the playground blacktop and metal slide reach over $140^\circ\text{F} / 60^\circ\text{C}$—hot enough to cause skin burns in seconds! Meanwhile, running across the court makes your body pump sweat, but resting under a dense campus oak or mesquite tree instantly stops the overheating. How does our body regulate its temperature, and how do campus shade trees act as life-saving public health shields?
> 
> **Essential Question:** *How do human thermoregulation, hydration, and living tree canopies work together to protect us from dangerous South Texas heat?*

---

### 2. Learning Objectives & Success Criteria
- **Health/PE Objective:** Students will identify the critical playground burn threshold ($104^\circ\text{F} / 40^\circ\text{C}$), memorize the 3 red-flag symptoms of heat exhaustion (dizziness, nausea, pale/clammy skin), and calculate personal water intake needs ($250\text{ to }500\text{ mL} \approx 8\text{ to }16\text{ fl oz}$ per 30 minutes of outdoor play).
- **Science Objective:** Students will conduct an infrared temperature audit of 5 distinct campus surfaces and model evaporative cooling using wet vs. dry thermal probes.
- **Math Objective:** Students will calculate exact thermal deltas ($\Delta T$) in both Celsius and Fahrenheit ($^\circ\text{C} / ^\circ\text{F}$) and plot a 5-zone comparison bar graph.
- **ELAR Objective:** Students will compose a 3-part Claim-Evidence-Reasoning Recess Safety Plan.

### 5. 5E Instructional Learning Sequence

#### Phase 1: ENGAGE (10 Minutes) — The Sweat & Shade Mystery Hook
1. **The Forearm Touch Test:** Gather students in the breezeway. Have students touch the inside of their forearm:
   - *"When you sprint across the court at recess, tiny droplets of water appear on your skin. Why? Sweat isn't just water—it's your body's built-in air-conditioning fluid! Today we test how sweating and tree shade save our lives."*
2. **Bilingual Vocabulary Chant:** Call-and-response:
   - *Thermoregulation = Termorregulación (Body keeping 98.6°F!)*
   - *Perspiration = Transpiración / Sudor (Cooling water on skin!)*
   - *Thermal Refuge = Refugio Térmico (Cool shade sanctuary!)*
   - *Hydration = Hidratación (Drinking clean water!)*
3. **Walk to Playground Stations:** Assign teams to starting stations.

#### Phase 2 & 3: EXPLORE & EXPLAIN (25 Minutes) — 3 Rotational Field Stations

##### Station 1: 5-Surface Infrared Burn Hazard Audit (`STA-IR-BURN-HAZARD` · 8 Min)
- **Laser Aiming Safety:** Remind students to aim IR guns straight down at the ground and never toward eyes.
- **5-Zone Audit (Science 5.12B / Health 5.3A):**
  - Record 2 trials for each microclimate surface:
    1. *Zone 1: Blacktop Basketball Asphalt:* _______ $^\circ\text{C}$ / _______ $^\circ\text{F}$
    2. *Zone 2: Metal Playground Slide / Bench:* _______ $^\circ\text{C}$ / _______ $^\circ\text{F}$
    3. *Zone 3: Synthetic Rubber Turf / Mat:* _______ $^\circ\text{C}$ / _______ $^\circ\text{F}$
    4. *Zone 4: Unshaded Lawn Grass (Full Sun):* _______ $^\circ\text{C}$ / _______ $^\circ\text{F}$
    5. *Zone 5: Shaded Grass Under Microforest Tree:* _______ $^\circ\text{C}$ / _______ $^\circ\text{F}$
- **Burn Danger Evaluation:** Highlight that surfaces above $40^\circ\text{C} / 104^\circ\text{F}$ cause pain and contact burns within seconds on bare skin!

##### Station 2: Wet Sponge Evaporative Cooling Model (`STA-SWEAT-EVAPORATION-MODEL` · 8 Min)
- **The Simulated Sweat Experiment (Science 5.12B & 5.5B):**
  - Probe A (Dry Probe): Left in open ambient air. Starting Temp: _______ $^\circ\text{C}$ / _______ $^\circ\text{F}$.
  - Probe B (Sweat Probe): Mist with water spray and fan gently for 60 seconds. Ending Temp: _______ $^\circ\text{C}$ / _______ $^\circ\text{F}$.
- **Phase-Change Mechanism:**
  - When water changes from liquid to gas (evaporation), it absorbs latent heat from the probe (or human skin), causing the temperature to drop by $5^\circ\text{C} \text{ to } 10^\circ\text{C}$ ($9^\circ\text{F} \text{ to } 18^\circ\text{F}$)!
  - Note: High humidity slows evaporation, making shade even more critical for survival.

##### Station 3: Recess Hydration & Shade Safety Protocol (`STA-HYDRATION-SAFETY-PLAN` · 8 Min)
- **Fluid Replenishment Calculation (Math 5.4H):**
  - For every 30 minutes of vigorous physical activity in South Texas heat, an elementary student loses $250\text{ to }500\text{ mL}$ ($8.5\text{ to }17\text{ fl oz}$) of water through sweat.
  - Calculate team water needs: If 4 teammates play for 1 hour:
    $$\text{Total Water Needed} = 4 \times 500\text{ mL} = 2{,}000\text{ mL (2.0 Liters / 67.6 fl oz)}$$
- **Heat Illness Symptom Matrix:** Memorize the 3 Warning Signals:
  - ⚠️ *Signal 1:* Dizziness or headache (*Mareo o dolor de cabeza*)
  - ⚠️ *Signal 2:* Heavy sweating with cold, clammy pale skin (*Sudoración excesiva con piel fría*)
  - ⚠️ *Signal 3:* Nausea or extreme fatigue (*Náuseas o fatiga extrema*)
  - *Immediate Action:* Move student to deep tree shade, elevate feet, sip cool water, notify adult!

#### Phase 4: ELABORATE (5 Minutes) — Whole-School Shade Equity & Recess Rules
- Gather in the microforest shade circle:
  - *"If the asphalt is 135°F and the tree shade is 88°F, what rule should our school enforce for August and September recess?"*
  - Rule: Mandatory 5-minute shade and hydration breaks every 20 minutes of outdoor play!

#### Phase 5: EVALUATE (5 Minutes) — Recess Heat Safety CER Synthesis
- Students complete the printable Field Lab Worksheet, completing the bar graph and CER safety action paragraph.
- Return equipment and line up for classroom return.

## III. Misconception Alerts & Differentiation Scaffolds

- ❌ *Misconception:* "Sweating makes you hot because you feel warm when you sweat."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Sweating is the cooling response triggered when your core temperature rises; the evaporation of that sweat actively removes heat from your body.
- ❌ *Misconception:* "Drinking ice-cold water only when you feel super thirsty is enough."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* By the time you feel thirsty, your body is already 1–2% dehydrated. Drink water before, during, and after outdoor play.
- 💡 *Scaffolding for ELLs:* Use visual emoji temperature danger scales (🟢 Green Safe $<90^\circ\text{F}$, 🟡 Yellow Caution $90\text{–}103^\circ\text{F}$, 🔴 Red Danger $>104^\circ\text{F}$).
- 🚀 *Advanced Extension (GT):* Research Wet-Bulb Globe Temperature (WBGT) and explain why military bases and athletic leagues monitor humidity alongside temperature.
