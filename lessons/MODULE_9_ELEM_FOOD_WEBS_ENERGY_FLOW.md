## II. Master Teacher Instructional Guide (5E Cycle)

### 1. Essential Question & Phenomenon
> **Anchor Phenomenon:** On a warm South Texas afternoon, green Honey Mesquite and Texas Ebony leaves capture bright sunlight. Caterpillars munch on the leaves, Green Jays and Spiny Lizards hunt the caterpillars, and Red-tailed Hawks circle above. When leaves and creatures drop to the ground, beetles and fungi turn them back into rich soil. Where does all the energy in our schoolyard come from, and how does it connect every living creature like a woven web?
> 
> **Essential Question:** *How does radiant energy from the sun flow through living producers, consumers, and decomposers in our campus microforest food web?*

---

### 2. Learning Objectives & Success Criteria
- **Science Objective:** Students will identify and classify schoolyard organisms into 4 distinct trophic categories: Producers (*Productores*), Primary Consumers (*Consumidores Primarios*), Secondary Consumers (*Consumidores Secundarios*), and Decomposers (*Descomponedores*), mapping the continuous transfer of energy.
- **Math Objective:** Students will calculate 10% trophic energy transfer steps using dual metric/imperial biomass estimates ($\text{kg / lbs}$ and calories/joules).
- **Social Studies/ELAR Objective:** Students will write a structured Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER) argument detailing how protecting native campus trees preserves local food webs.
- **Health Objective:** Students will link plant photosynthesis to human dietary energy and nutrient cycles.

### 5. 5E Instructional Learning Sequence

#### Phase 1: ENGAGE (10 Minutes) — The Solar Spark Hook
1. **The Sunlight Hand-Trap:** Gather students at the microforest threshold. Have each student cup their hands toward the sun and then touch a green leaf:
   - *"Where did the energy inside your breakfast come from this morning? If you ate eggs, the chicken ate grain, and the grain caught sunlight. Every bite of food on Earth is solar energy in disguise!"*
2. **Bilingual Vocabulary Chant:** Call-and-response:
   - *Producer = Productor (Makes food from sun!)*
   - *Primary Consumer = Consumidor Primario (Eats plants!)*
   - *Secondary Consumer = Consumidor Secundario (Eats animals!)*
   - *Decomposer = Descomponedor (Recycles matter!)*
3. **Walk to Microforest Stations:** Assign teams to starting stations.

#### Phase 2 & 3: EXPLORE & EXPLAIN (25 Minutes) — 3 Rotational Field Stations

##### Station 1: Primary Producer & Solar Flux Audit (`STA-PRODUCER-SOLAR-AUDIT` · 8 Min)
- **Solar Input vs. Leaf Temperature:** Aim the IR thermometer at an open sunlit leaf ($31^\circ\text{C} / 88^\circ\text{F}$) versus a shaded understory leaf ($26^\circ\text{C} / 79^\circ\text{F}$).
- **Chlorophyll Solar Trapping:** Use the 10x hand lens to inspect the top and bottom of Honey Mesquite (*Mezquite Dulce*) or Texas Ebony (*Ébano*) leaflets. Identify the bright green chlorophyll capturing radiant solar energy ($E_{\text{solar}}$).
- **Biomass Estimation:** Estimate the leaf cluster mass ($50\text{ g} \approx 1.76\text{ oz}$) producing plant sugar ($C_6H_{12}O_6$) via photosynthesis.

##### Station 2: Consumer Trophic Link Tally & 10% Rule (`STA-CONSUMER-TROPHIC-TALLY` · 8 Min)
- **Field Wildlife Search:** Quietly inspect branches, bark furrows, and open ground for 4 minutes:
  - *Herbivores / Consumidores Primarios:* Caterpillars, grasshoppers, seed beetles, cicadas.
  - *Carnivores & Omnivores / Consumidores Secundarios:* Green Jays (*Chara Verde*), Spiny Lizards (*Lagartija Espinosa*), ladybugs, spiders.
- **The 10% Energy Pyramid Calculation (Math 5.4H):**
  - If the Mesquite Tree captures $10{,}000\text{ calories}$ ($41{,}840\text{ J}$) of solar energy:
  - Primary Consumers receive: $10{,}000 \times 0.10 = 1{,}000\text{ calories}$ ($4{,}184\text{ J}$).
  - Secondary Consumers receive: $1{,}000 \times 0.10 = 100\text{ calories}$ ($418.4\text{ J}$).
  - Apex Predator receives: $100 \times 0.10 = 10\text{ calories}$ ($41.84\text{ J}$).

##### Station 3: Decomposer Soil Recycler Core (`STA-DECOMPOSER-SOIL-CORE` · 8 Min)
- **Leaf Litter Inspection:** Kneel at the mulch base under the canopy. Gently part the top dry leaves to reveal the dark, moist humus layer below ($5\text{ cm} / 2\text{ inches}$ deep).
- **Recycler Inventory:** Use hand lenses to tally visible decomposers: pillbugs/woodlice (*cochinillas*), earthworms (*lombrices*), fungal mycelium threads (*hifas de hongos*), and shelf fungi.
- **Matter Cycling Principle (Science 5.12B & 5.5E):** Decomposers break down dead wood and fallen leaves, releasing stored nitrogen and phosphorus minerals back into the soil for tree roots to absorb again. Matter cycles endlessly!

#### Phase 4: ELABORATE (5 Minutes) — The Yarn "Web of Life" Ripple
- Form a quick whole-class standing circle.
- Pass a spool of green yarn from the "Sun" student to "Mesquite Tree", then to "Caterpillar", "Spiny Lizard", and "Fungus".
- Tug the yarn gently: *"When drought or deforestation cuts down one native tree, who feels the tug?"* Every student in the web feels the physical vibration!

#### Phase 5: EVALUATE (5 Minutes) — CER Synthesis & Clipboard Check
- Students record their CER summary on the worksheet and verify all data boxes.
- Return clipboards and line up for classroom return.

## III. Misconception Alerts & Differentiation Scaffolds

- ❌ *Misconception:* "Carnivores get their energy only from other animals, not from the sun."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* All animal energy comes indirectly from the sun. The lizard eats the grasshopper, but the grasshopper's body was built entirely from sun-powered grass.
- ❌ *Misconception:* "Decomposers just eat trash and are not part of the food web."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Decomposers are the vital cleanup crew that closes the loop, recycling dead organic matter into nutrients so new plants can grow.
- 💡 *Scaffolding for Emerging Bilinguals:* Utilize visual food chain arrows labeled with bilingual terms (*Sun $\rightarrow$ Producer / Productor $\rightarrow$ Herbivore / Hervíboro $\rightarrow$ Carnivore / Carnívoro $\rightarrow$ Decomposer / Descomponedor*).
- 🚀 *Advanced Extension (GT):* Calculate how many kilograms ($1\text{ kg} \approx 2.2\text{ lbs}$) of mesquite foliage are required to support a 500-gram ($1.1\text{ lb}$) Texas Spiny Lizard through the trophic pyramid.
