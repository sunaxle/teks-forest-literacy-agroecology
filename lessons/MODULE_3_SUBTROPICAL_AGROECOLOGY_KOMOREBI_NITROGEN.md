## II. Master Teacher Instructional Guide (5E Cycle)

### 1. Essential Question & Phenomenon
> **Anchor Phenomenon:** In the blistering, semi-arid climate of South Texas, how do fragile, shade-loving understory crops like wild chile piquin (*Capsicum annuum*) and native heirloom vegetables thrive directly beneath thorny trees like Honey Mesquite (*Prosopis glandulosa*) and Texas Ebony (*Ebenopsis ebano*) without artificial synthetic fertilizer or full-sun scorching—and how does the dappled sunlight (*Komorebi*) optimize photosynthetic efficiency while preventing photodamage?
> 
> **Essential Question:** *How do native leguminous trees architect microclimatic light regimes and biological nitrogen pumps to sustain high-yield, drought-resilient agroecological polycultures?*

---

### 2. Learning Objectives & Success Criteria
- **Biology / APES Objective:** Students will sample and analyze leguminous root nodules for leghaemoglobin activity, calculate the Beer-Lambert light extinction coefficient ($I = I_0 e^{-k \cdot \text{LAI}}$) using PAR light meters, and quantify annual biological nitrogen fixation (kg N/ha/yr).
- **Agroecology Objective:** Students will evaluate a 4-tier vertical polyculture architecture (Overstory canopy, Midstory subcanopy, Herbaceous shrub layer, Rhizosphere root zone) to model closed-loop nutrient and moisture cycling.
- **Fine Arts Objective:** Students will execute high-contrast chiaroscuro botanical field sketches capturing bipinnate leaf anatomy, pulvini sleep movements, and the tonal geometry of dappled canopy light (*Komorebi*).

### 4. Safety & Botanical Field Protocols
- **Sharps Safety:** Students must use caution when slicing root nodules with dissecting scalpels. Always cut away from the body onto the dissecting tray.
- **Root Preservation Protocol:** When extracting legume root nodules, perform micro-sampling on secondary lateral roots only. Do not damage primary taproots. Backfill all soil cores immediately after observation.
- **Thorn Awareness:** Tamaulipan thornscrub species (Mesquite, Tenaza, Catclaw) have sharp spines; students must wear leather gardening gloves during root and canopy inspections.

### 5. 5E Instructional Learning Sequence

#### Phase 1: ENGAGE (15 Minutes) — The Green Revolution vs The Ancient Thornscrub
1. **The Nitrogen Dilemma:** Present two contrasting agricultural models: (A) An industrial monoculture cornfield requiring 150 lbs/acre of synthetic fossil-fuel-derived Haber-Bosch nitrogen fertilizer, vulnerable to catastrophic runoff and soil acidification; vs (B) A traditional Indigenous/Mesoamerican agroforestry system where crops grow beneath nitrogen-fixing legume canopies with zero synthetic inputs.
2. **The Phenomenon of "Komorebi" (木漏れ日):** Introduce the Japanese concept of *Komorebi*—the dynamic, filtered sunlight streaming through tree foliage. Explain that full midday desert sun ($2000\text{ }\mu\text{mol}\cdot\text{m}^{-2}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}\text{ PAR}$) causes **photoinhibition** (severe photodamage to chlorophyll Photosystem II enzymes), whereas dappled canopy light provides the optimal light saturation point ($400-800\text{ }\mu\text{mol}\cdot\text{m}^{-2}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$) with reduced leaf heat stress.

#### Phase 2: EXPLORE (40 Minutes) — Nitrogen Bioassay & PAR Profile
1. **Vertical PAR Canopy Profile (Science 1.A / Bio 12.B):**
   - Record Incident Solar PAR ($I_0$) in full unshaded sun.
   - Record Filtered Understory PAR ($I$) at 3 vertical heights: Top of Shrub Layer (1.5 m), Midstory Leaf Level (0.75 m), and Soil Surface (0.0 m).
   - Compute Canopy Light Transmittance: $T = I \div I_0 \times 100\%$.
2. **Rhizobial Root Nodule Bioassay:**
   - Gently excavate a 10 cm root core from beneath a tagged Honey Mesquite (*Prosopis glandulosa*) or Texas Ebony (*Ebenopsis ebano*).
   - Locate spherical or coralloid root nodules attached to secondary roots.
   - Rinse gently with distilled water and place on the dissecting tray.
   - Bisect the nodule with the scalpel:
     * **Pink / Ruby Red Core:** Active nitrogenase enzyme protected by oxygen-buffering **leghaemoglobin** (Fixing active atmospheric $N_2 \to NH_4^+$).
     * **White / Pale Green Core:** Immature or inactive nodule.
     * **Brown / Black Core:** Senescent / decomposing nodule.
3. **Soil Nitrate ($\text{NO}_3^-$) Assay:** Compare soil nitrate levels in the top 5 cm of soil directly beneath the legume canopy vs 10 meters away in open turf grass.

#### Phase 3: EXPLAIN (25 Minutes) — Biochemical Nitrogen Cycling & Beer-Lambert Physics
1. **Biochemical Fixation Reaction:**
   $$\text{N}_2 + 8\text{H}^+ + 8\text{e}^- + 16\text{ATP} \xrightarrow{\text{Nitrogenase}} 2\text{NH}_3 + \text{H}_2 + 16\text{ADP} + 16\text{P}_i$$
   Explain how the tree supplies photosynthetic malate/glucose sugars to the bacteroids, while the bacteria deliver bioavailable ammonium to the host plant and surrounding soil rhizosphere.
2. **Beer-Lambert Canopy Extinction Modeling:**
   $$I = I_0 \cdot e^{-k \cdot \text{LAI}}$$
   Where $k \approx 0.55$ (extinction coefficient for bipinnate thornscrub) and $\text{LAI}$ is the Leaf Area Index. Calculate the theoretical understory light intensity and compare with field sensor readings.

#### Phase 4: ELABORATE (25 Minutes) — Fine Arts Chiaroscuro & Agroforest Design
1. **Komorebi Chiaroscuro Field Drawing (Fine Arts 4.2C):**
   - Students execute a 20-minute observational sketch in their field notebooks.
   - Use graphite pencils (HB to 6B) to construct a 5-step tonal value scale (White highlight $\to$ Light gray $\to$ Mid-tone $\to$ Deep shadow $\to$ Core black).
   - Render the interlocking geometry of compound bipinnate leaflets and the delicate, organic sunfleck patterns cast across the soil and understory vegetation.
2. **Agroecological System Architecture:** Design a 3-tier polyculture layout for a 20x20 meter campus agroforest plot integrating canopy legumes, midstory fruit trees (Texas Persimmon), and edible shade-tolerant understory crops (Chile Piquin, native squash).

#### Phase 5: EVALUATE (15 Minutes) — Comprehensive Synthesis & Peer Critique
- Students complete the printable Field Lab Worksheet, compiling their PAR extinction calculations, root nodule bioassay observations, botanical chiaroscuro sketch, and evidence-based agroforestry design argument.

### 6. Misconception Alerts & Differentiation Scaffolds
- ❌ *Misconception:* "Plants absorb nitrogen gas ($N_2$) directly through their leaf stomata from the air."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Atmospheric $N_2$ has an extremely strong triple covalent bond ($N \equiv N$) that eukaryotic plants cannot break. Only prokaryotic diazotrophic bacteria possessing the nitrogenase enzyme can fix $N_2$ into bioavailable forms.
- ❌ *Misconception:* "More direct sunlight is always better for crop growth."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* In extreme heat (>95°F), excessive direct irradiance exceeds the photosynthetic light saturation point, causing stomatal closure, severe water stress, and photoinhibition. Dappled canopy shade (*Komorebi*) balances photon absorption with thermal preservation.
- 🌐 **Bilingual / ELL Scaffolding:** Provide Spanish scientific glossaries (*Fijación Biológica de Nitrógeno / Biological Nitrogen Fixation*, *Nódulos Radiculares / Root Nodules*, *Leghemoglobina / Leghaemoglobin*, *Fotoinhibición / Photoinhibition*, *Policultivo / Polyculture*).
- 🚀 **Advanced / APES Extension:** Calculate the total annual monetary fertilizer savings: If a 1-hectare agroforest fixes $75\text{ kg N/ha/yr}$ of nitrogen, calculate the equivalent mass of commercial synthetic urea ($46\%\text{ N}$) displaced and the avoided metric tons of greenhouse gas emissions ($\text{CO}_2\text{e}$).
