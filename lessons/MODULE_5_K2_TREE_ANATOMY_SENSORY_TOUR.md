## II. Master Teacher Instructional Guide (5E Cycle)

### 1. Essential Question & Phenomenon
> **Anchor Phenomenon:** A tree stands tall in the South Texas wind and scorching heat without falling over or drying out. What special body parts help the tree drink water from deep underground, stand sturdy, wear armor, and make its own food in the sky?
> 
> **Essential Question:** *What are the four main parts of a campus tree, and how do they work together like a living green team?*

---

### 2. Learning Objectives & Success Criteria
- **Science Objective:** Students will touch, observe, and correctly identify the 4 primary anatomical parts of a tree: Roots (*Raíces*), Trunk (*Tronco*), Bark (*Corteza*), and Leaves/Crown (*Hojas/Copa*).
- **Math Objective:** Students will measure the trunk circumference of two tree species using non-standard ribbon links and dual standard units ($\text{cm / in}$).
- **ELAR/Fine Arts Objective:** Students will complete a labeled "Tree Anatomy Passport" with bilingual labels and draw their favorite campus tree part.
- **Health Objective:** Students will feel and compare tree shade versus direct sunlight.

### 5. 5E Instructional Learning Sequence

#### Phase 1: ENGAGE (10 Minutes) — The "Be a Tree" Kinesthetic Warm-Up
1. **Kinesthetic Modeling:** Stand in a circle in the courtyard. Lead students in the "Tree Body" movement:
   - *"Toes wiggle in the soil like ROOTS drinking water!" (Wiggle feet).*
   - *"Body stands strong and straight like a sturdy TRUNK!" (Stand tall).*
   - *"Arms stretch wide into the blue sky like BRANCHES and CROWN!" (Spread arms).*
   - *"Fingers flutter like green LEAVES making sugar!" (Flutter fingers).*
   - *"Skin protects us from the sun just like BARK protects the wood!" (Touch arms).*
2. **Bilingual Vocabulary Chant:** Call-and-response: *Roots = Raíces! Trunk = Tronco! Bark = Corteza! Leaves = Hojas!*
3. **Walk to Microforest:** Walk as quiet "Tree Detectives" to the tagged campus trees.

#### Phase 2 & 3: EXPLORE & EXPLAIN (25 Minutes) — 2 Field Stations

##### Station 1: Root-to-Crown Tactile Walk (`STA-ROOT-CROWN-WALK` · 12 Min)
- **Root Zone (*Raíces*):** Kneel down safely. Look for surface roots spreading out from the base of a Honey Mesquite or Cedar Elm. Use hand lenses to inspect how roots grip the soil like strong fingers.
- **Trunk & Bark Zone (*Tronco y Corteza*):** Gently touch the bark with flat palms. Is it bumpy, rough, or smooth? Explain that bark is the tree's sunscreen and jacket!
- **Crown & Leaf Zone (*Copa y Hojas*):** Look up into the canopy (*dosel*). Observe how leaves catch sunlight and dance in the breeze.

##### Station 2: Trunk Hugging & Girth Measurement (`STA-TRUNK-HUG-MEASURE` · 13 Min)
- **The Tree Hug Test:** Students gently hug the tree trunk. Can their arms wrap all the way around? (1 hug, 2 student hugs, or half a hug?).
- **Dual-Unit Ribbon Measurement:** Wrap the measuring tape around the trunk at chest height ($1\text{ meter} \approx 3.3\text{ feet}$ above ground).
- Record trunk circumference:
  - Tree 1 (e.g., Honey Mesquite): _____ $\text{cm}$ / _____ $\text{inches}$
  - Tree 2 (e.g., Young Cedar Elm): _____ $\text{cm}$ / _____ $\text{inches}$
- **Anatomy Sketch:** In the passport box, students sketch the tree and draw lines connecting each word to its matching tree part.

#### Phase 4: ELABORATE (5 Minutes) — Shade vs. Sun Touch Comparison
- Teacher takes an IR thermometer reading of the open playground blacktop ($50^\circ\text{C} / 122^\circ\text{F}$) and the shaded grass beneath the tree ($32^\circ\text{C} / 90^\circ\text{F}$).
- Discuss: *"How does the tree crown protect our bodies during recess?"*

#### Phase 5: EVALUATE (5 Minutes) — Passport Check & Return
- Students complete the 2-question pictorial check.
- High-five their tree partner and line up for the indoor return.

## III. Misconception Alerts & Differentiation Scaffolds

- ❌ *Misconception:* "Tree bark is dead and doesn't matter."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Just beneath the outer bark is the living phloem and cambium layer that carries food throughout the entire tree. Harming bark hurts the tree's circulation.
- ❌ *Misconception:* "Roots only hold the tree up; they don't do anything else."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Roots have thousands of microscopic root hairs that absorb gallons of water and minerals from underground soil every single day.
- 💡 *Scaffolding for K-1 Readers:* Use visual icons (leaf icon, root icon, ruler icon) and Spanish cognates alongside all English terms.
