## II. Master Teacher Instructional Guide (5E Cycle)

### 1. Essential Question & Phenomenon
> **Anchor Phenomenon:** On a 95°F afternoon in South Texas, why does an asphalt basketball court feel like a sizzling frying pan (over 135°F), while the grass under a Honey Mesquite or Cedar Elm feels cool and refreshing—and why does a sealed plastic bag tied around a tree branch quickly fill with clean water drops?
> 
> **Essential Question:** *How do campus trees act as biological water-pumps and natural air-conditioners to protect our school community from extreme urban heat?*

---

### 2. Learning Objectives & Success Criteria
- **Science Objective:** Students will collect, measure, and analyze quantitative temperature and transpiration data across 5 distinct campus micro-habitats to prove that living trees actively transfer thermal energy via phase-change evaporative cooling.
- **Math Objective:** Students will calibrate their personal walking step pace, calculate the cooling shade area ($A \approx 3.14 \times r^2$) and perimeter of campus trees, and compute the total square meters of thermal refuge available to students during recess.
- **Health Objective:** Students will correlate surface heat levels with human physiological thermal safety limits, creating an action plan for hydration and recess heat protection.

### 4. Safety, Heat Precautions & Outdoor Stewardship
- **Heat Safety (Health 5.3A):** Conduct investigations during morning or early afternoon hours before peak heat index. All students must wear hats/sunscreen and carry water bottles.
- **Eye Safety:** Remind students NEVER to aim the laser pointer of infrared thermometers at anyone's eyes or faces.
- **Gentle Plant Handling:** When attaching transpiration bags, select healthy sunlit leaf clusters; do not break, snap, or strip branches. Remove all plastic bags and ties before returning indoors.

---

### 5. 5E Instructional Learning Sequence

#### Phase 1: ENGAGE (15 Minutes) — The Asphalt Frying Pan Hook
1. **The Touch Test Analogy:** Gather students at the edge of the schoolyard blacktop. Ask: *"If you walked barefoot across this blacktop right now, what would happen? Why does a canvas shade umbrella feel hot, but resting under an oak or mesquite feels like an outdoor air-conditioner?"*
2. **Concept Anchor:** Introduce the term **Transpiration**—explain that a tree is a living solar-powered pump that drinks water deep from the soil and exhales invisible water vapor through microscopic leaf nostrils called **stomata**.
3. **Setup Bag Experiment:** Walk to the campus microforest. Guide each group to slide a clear 1-gallon plastic bag over a leafy cluster of sunlit leaves (approx. 20–30 leaves) on a tagged campus tree (e.g., Honey Mesquite, Cedar Elm, Mexican Olive, or Live Oak). Seal tightly with a zip-tie. Note the starting timestamp.

#### Phase 2: EXPLORE (35 Minutes) — The Campus Microclimate Transect & Pacing Audit
1. **Pacing Calibration (Math 5.4H):** Along a 10-meter tape measure laid on the sidewalk, each student walks 10 natural strides, records the distance, and calculates their individual **Pace Factor** ($\text{Pace} = \text{Meters} \div 10$).
2. **Canopy Shadow Radius Measurement:** Students locate their team's tagged tree and measure the shadow radius in four directions (North, South, East, West) by counting strides and multiplying by their pace factor.
3. **6-Zone Infrared Surface Temperature Audit:** Using the IR thermometer, students record 3 repeated surface temperature readings at each of the 6 campus microclimate zones:
   - *Zone 1:* Unshaded Blacktop Asphalt (Basketball Court / Parking Lot)
   - *Zone 2:* Unshaded Synthetic Turf or Rubber Playground Surface
   - *Zone 3:* Unshaded Open Lawn Grass (Full Sun)
   - *Zone 4:* Shaded Grass / Mulch beneath Microforest Tree Canopy
   - *Zone 5:* Living Tree Trunk Bark (North & South faces)
   - *Zone 6:* Living Tree Leaves in Canopy Crown

#### Phase 3: EXPLAIN (20 Minutes) — Stomata Micro-Inspect & Phase-Change Cooling
1. **Transpiration Harvest:** Return to the sealed transpiration bags after 45–60 minutes. Observe the condensation fog and water droplets that pooled inside the bottom corner of the bag.
2. **Quantitative Measurement:** Carefully untie the bag, drain the accumulated water into a 10 mL graduated cylinder, and record the exact volume in milliliters ($\text{mL}$).
3. **Stomata Hand-Lens Inspection:** Pluck one fallen leaf from the ground (or inspect an attached leaf with a 10x hand lens). Observe the tiny stomatal openings on the underside of the leaf blade.
4. **Energy Transfer Mechanism (Science 5.12B & 5.5A):** Explain that when liquid water changes into invisible water vapor (evaporation), it **absorbs heat energy** from the surrounding air. Just as sweat cools human skin, transpiration actively extracts heat from the schoolyard!

#### Phase 4: ELABORATE (15 Minutes) — Math Canopy Area & Health Heat Refuge
1. **Cooling Footprint Calculation:** Students calculate the total square meters of shade provided by their tree using the scaffolded formula: $\text{Area} = 3.14 \times (r_{\text{avg}})^2$.
2. **Recess Heat Safety Matrix (Health 5.3A):** Compare the surface temperatures against the Safe Surface Threshold for children ($104^\circ\text{F} / 40^\circ\text{C}$). Students determine whether asphalt or playground surfaces exceed burn danger thresholds and calculate how many students can safely stand within the tree's cool shade zone (allow approx. 1.5 $\text{m}^2$ per student).

#### Phase 5: EVALUATE (15 Minutes) — Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER) Synthesis
- Students complete the printable Field Lab Worksheet, compiling their data table, bar graph comparison, and writing an evidence-based CER paragraph answering: *"How do campus trees alter microclimate energy flow to protect student health?"*

---

### 6. Misconception Alerts & Differentiation Scaffolds
- ❌ *Misconception:* "Tree shade is cool just because it blocks sunlight like a wooden roof or tent."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* Inert shade (tents/umbrellas) only blocks light; the surface underneath still traps sensible heat. Living trees provide **active biological evaporative cooling** through stomatal transpiration, cooling ambient air by an extra 5°F to 9°F beyond shade alone.
- ❌ *Misconception:* "The water inside the plastic bag came from rain or morning dew."  
  ✔️ *Correction:* The dry bag was sealed around dry leaves on a sunny day; the liquid water condensed exclusively from vapor exhaled by internal xylem-to-stomata transport.
- 🌐 **Bilingual / ELL Scaffolding:** Provide Spanish cognate word bank (*Transpiración / Transpiration*, *Estomas / Stomata*, *Microclima / Microclimate*, *Evaporación / Evaporation*, *Albedo / Refletividad*). Pair students strategically and provide tactile step-by-step visual cue cards.
- 🚀 **Advanced / GT Extension:** Calculate the schoolyard's **Total Water Pump Capacity**: If 1 mature shade tree transpires 40 gallons of water per day, calculate how many liters of cooling water the entire 20-tree campus microforest pumps into the school atmosphere every week.
